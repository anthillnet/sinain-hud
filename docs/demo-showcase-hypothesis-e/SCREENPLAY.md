# Sinain demo - HP-E: protect the human's attention

## Creative decision

The demo tests the actual HP-E promise:

> **Agents can work in parallel. Human attention cannot. Sinain protects the human's flow by making every return cheap, timely, and voluntary.**

HP-E targets people running several agent sessions in parallel. The differentiator is the cost of steering and re-entering concurrent work, not the number of tools. The product site's sequence remains the interaction grammar: **It notices. It asks. Your agents join.**

This is a behavioral hypothesis, not a validated benefit. The film demonstrates the proposed interaction and faster orientation; it does not claim proven productivity or retention outcomes.

## The scenario

Maya has three agents working in parallel:

- a CLI agent debugging checkout webhooks,
- a chat agent revising a launch deck,
- a research agent comparing pricing pages.

She leaves the checkout thread to answer an interruption. When she returns, the terminal has moved on and she cannot remember what decision the agent needs. Sinain offers a quiet re-entry brief at the return moment: goal, last decision, current blocker, and one next step. Maya taps **Resume with agent**; the agent receives the same brief and continues without making her reconstruct the session. At the next natural breakpoint, Sinain offers an ignorable pause rather than more work.

The emotional before/after is **re-entry dread -> orientation -> one deliberate next action**.

## Shared visual language

- 16:9, 1920 x 1080, 30 fps, deep navy ground (`#0E1116`).
- Mathematical-explainer grammar: STIX serif statements, IBM Plex Mono evidence labels, thin grids, constructed lines, dots, braces, and transformations.
- Sinain green (`#1F8039`) means a user-approved action. Cyan (`#58C4DD`) describes agent work. Yellow (`#F4D35E`) marks human attention. Muted red (`#FC6255`) marks unresolved load or interruption.
- The central visual law is `agents = many; attention = one`. Agents may branch; the yellow attention point never duplicates.
- Motion explains cognition: context fragments accumulate during a thread, scatter on a switch, then compress into a re-entry brief. Avoid frantic notification montages, productivity-score dashboards, brain imagery, or claims to diagnose emotion.
- Live desktop footage appears inside a clean 16:9 inset with a 1 px green keyline. Animation labels real interaction but does not redraw product UI.
- Sound: restrained felt-piano/marimba, soft graphite line sounds, a low breath-like release when the re-entry brief resolves. No notification ping and no alarm on the pause offer.

### 60-second narration, clean copy

> Parallel agents can make three kinds of progress at once. But the person steering them can actively guide only one thread at a time.
>
> Each switch leaves a little context behind: why this mattered, what changed, what the agent needs next. Coming back can feel harder than starting another task.
>
> Sinain notices the return and offers a quiet, evidence-based re-entry brief. Ignore it and it fades. Nothing resumes, tracks, or reaches an agent without her tap.
>
> Maya taps. Goal, last decision, current blocker, and next step return in one glance. She does not reconstruct the whole session. She only chooses the next move. The right agent rejoins with the same context, already briefed.
>
> And when more work would add load, Sinain can ask whether it is time to pause.
>
> The agents do the work. You keep your focus.

## Evidence and wording rules

- Say `left 18 min ago`, `test still failing`, or `agent waiting`; never infer a personal state or display a focus score.
- Use predictions to choose timing and surface size, not to turn uncertain behavior into a confident claim.
- The re-entry offer is silent, non-modal, self-dismissing, and opt-in.
- The pause offer appears only at a natural breakpoint. It must be easier to ignore than to interpret as a warning.
- The human remains the decision-maker; the film never celebrates maximum agent activity as the goal.

## What the demo proves - and does not prove

It demonstrates the HP-E concept: context-aware re-entry across parallel agent work, an explicit consent gate, shared briefing between human and agent, and an evidence-based pause offer. It does **not** prove reduced switching cost, improved productivity, retention, or demand.

The behavioral validation targets remain:

- re-entry-brief opt-in and repeat usage,
- self-reported reduction in switch/re-entry dread,
- time from return to a meaningful next action,
- pause-offer acceptance/dismissal rate,
- retention among multi-agent users,
- whether the feature reduces load instead of becoming another nagging surface.

## Acceptance criteria

- A viewer can explain `agents = many; attention = one` after one viewing.
- The before/after reads as **re-entry dread -> orientation -> one next action**.
- The user visibly chooses before the agent receives or acts on the brief.
- The re-entry card contains evidence and next action, not a focus score or diagnosis.
- The 20-second cut is derivable from the 60-second master without new artwork.
- At least one real product interaction appears in the 20-second cut; L1-L3 appear in the 60-second cut.
- Every on-screen sentence is readable at 1080p without pausing.
- `The agents do the work. You keep your focus.` is the final value statement in both cuts.
